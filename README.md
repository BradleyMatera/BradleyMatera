## What I do

I usually just call myself a **developer**.

I am not the person who is going to pretend I hand-write every system from memory or that having a large repository automatically makes me an expert in everything inside it. I use documentation, AI, debugging tools, testing, and whatever else helps me get from an idea to something that actually works.

What I am good at is **building the whole experience**. If something needs a frontend, an API, authentication, a database, deployment, forms, automation, or a cloud service to make the idea usable end to end, I tend to keep going until those pieces are connected.

A lot of that work is heavily AI-assisted. I direct the work, make decisions, test it, change it, break it, fix it, and learn from it, but I do not claim that AI-generated implementation means I personally have senior-level depth in every technology it touched.

That is also why my GitHub changes so much: I build a lot of finished experiments, demos, tools, websites, and prototypes because building the complete thing is how I learn what the pieces are actually for.

## Experiments and learning projects

These are projects I build because I want to understand what is possible. They can be large and technically ambitious, but many are heavily AI-generated and should be read as **experimentation**, not as a claim that I personally have expert-level mastery of every implementation detail.

<details>
<summary><strong>Scout / ProjectHub — AI systems experiment</strong></summary>
<br>

An ongoing experiment in making a useful assistant work with limited compute. The project currently explores retrieval, BM25, reciprocal-rank fusion, conversation state, deterministic evidence tools, response validation, Docker-based testing, and swappable inference providers.

I use this project to learn about AI system design and to test ideas. I do **not** claim that I invented or independently mastered every technique implemented in the repository.

**Repository:** [BradleyMatera/ProjectHub](https://github.com/BradleyMatera/ProjectHub)

</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>Interactive Systems Lab — browser / Babylon.js playground</strong></summary>
<br>

A large AI-assisted browser sandbox with 3D scenes, simulations, interface concepts, engineering visualizations, and playable demos. I built it because I wanted a place where I could keep trying completely different interaction ideas and actually click through the finished results.

**Live:** [Interactive Systems Lab](https://bradleymatera.github.io/GameTesting/)  
**Repository:** [BradleyMatera/GameTesting](https://github.com/BradleyMatera/GameTesting)

</details>
